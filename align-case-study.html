ALIGN / ORIGINAL CONCEPT CASE STUDY

A clearer path to a discovery call.

Design an application funnel for a fictional independent business coach. The goal is a considered enquiry, not the highest possible volume of calls.

Self-initiated concept · Fictional brand and audience · No client engagement or measured results

Try the Align interactive demo ↗

The person behind the click.

Independent founders who have a working offer but feel pulled between too many priorities. They need to understand the coaching approach and whether it fits their situation before sharing their details.

Scope
Strategy, page structure, visual direction, responsive concept build
Constraint
No real customer research, offer validation, or conversion data

The funnel map.

  1. Offer introduction
  2. Audience fit
  3. Coaching framework
  4. Questions
  5. Short application

This is a proposed journey. A real engagement would validate the audience assumptions and offer facts before design.

Why each section exists.

01

Offer introduction

The first screen names the desired direction without guaranteeing a business result. One CTA moves visitors into the detail.

02

Audience fit

Specific outcomes—clarity, offer direction, next steps—help a founder decide whether the conversation is relevant.

03

Coaching framework

Reflect, focus, and plan gives an intangible service a structure visitors can understand.

04

Questions

Fit and next-step answers reduce uncertainty before the application. Credentials and approved proof would be added only when available.

05

Application

An email and one question keep the first step short. The confirmation should explain review and scheduling, rather than imply an instant booking.

From structure to visual direction.

I started with the order of decisions: relevance, fit, approach, then action. The wireframe gives the offer the most space and keeps one primary action. In the final direction, restrained typography and a warm palette support a calm, considered conversation.

One journey. Two screen sizes.

On desktop, generous margins give the headline room. On a narrow screen, the same reading order becomes one column, the headline scales down, and inputs fill the available width. The CTA stays in the normal page flow so it does not cover content.

What I would A/B test.

These are hypotheses, not results. With sufficient traffic, I would agree on a primary metric, run one change at a time, and account for traffic quality and uncertainty before drawing conclusions.

CTA wording

“Find your direction” versus “Explore coaching”.

Primary measure: Qualified application completion.

Compare clarity of intent without suggesting an immediate booking.

Application length

Email plus one question versus email alone.

Primary measure: Qualified call rate after application.

Track completion and lead quality together; a shorter form is not automatically better.

What would happen next?

Confirm the offer and audience through discovery, replace concept content with verified facts, connect delivery and contact tools, then evaluate the live journey. This portfolio concept demonstrates design reasoning; it does not establish conversion uplift.

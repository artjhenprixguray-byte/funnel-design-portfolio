FIELDNOTES / ORIGINAL CONCEPT CASE STUDY

Lead with usefulness.

Create a fictional opt-in funnel for a 90-day planning guide. The goal is to communicate the resource’s value before asking for an email.

Self-initiated concept · Fictional brand and audience · No client engagement or measured results

Try the Fieldnotes interactive demo ↗

The person behind the click.

Solo service providers who want a manageable way to choose one priority and make progress. They are likely to scan on mobile and want to know what they will receive.

Scope
Strategy, page structure, visual direction, responsive concept build
Constraint
No real customer research, offer validation, or conversion data

The funnel map.

  1. Resource promise
  2. Practical benefits
  3. Guide context
  4. Privacy questions
  5. Opt-in and next step

This is a proposed journey. A real engagement would validate the audience assumptions and offer facts before design.

Why each section exists.

01

Resource promise

Name the resource and the practical purpose early. The offer stays narrow enough to understand at a glance.

02

Practical benefits

Priority, milestones, and reflection make the guide concrete without invented productivity statistics.

03

Guide context

Explain why a simple planning exercise may be useful. This builds a bridge between a headline and the ask.

04

Privacy questions

Explain delivery expectations and demo limitations before asking for an email. A live version would state the actual consent and email policy.

05

Opt-in and next step

Ask for only the information needed. The concept confirmation offers a sample exercise, clearly separated from a real download or subscription.

From structure to visual direction.

The wireframe keeps the benefit explanation close to the opt-in, with no competing offers. Sage and paper tones reinforce the quiet planning theme. The homepage preview makes the resource tangible; the interactive page concentrates on the reading and form flow.

One journey. Two screen sizes.

The form uses a readable email input and a clearly labeled action. On desktop the text width is constrained for scanning; on mobile it becomes a single column without sideways scrolling. The confirmation stays near the form and is announced to assistive technology.

What I would A/B test.

These are hypotheses, not results. With sufficient traffic, I would agree on a primary metric, run one change at a time, and account for traffic quality and uncertainty before drawing conclusions.

Benefit order

Priority-setting first versus weekly reflection first.

Primary measure: Confirmed opt-ins per visitor.

Test the audience’s motivation while keeping the resource and delivery unchanged.

Resource preview

A guide cover versus an interior sample.

Primary measure: Confirmed opt-ins and download engagement.

Measure useful follow-through, not only form submissions.

What would happen next?

Confirm the offer and audience through discovery, replace concept content with verified facts, connect delivery and contact tools, then evaluate the live journey. This portfolio concept demonstrates design reasoning; it does not establish conversion uplift.

Forma: the thinking behind the page.

Help a creator understand a brand foundations course before previewing checkout.

This is a self-initiated design exercise. No client, testimonial, live conversion result or commercial relationship is claimed.

Explore the interactive Forma demo ↗

The brief and audience

Independent creators who have an idea but need a more consistent visual and verbal identity.

Funnel map

Course proposition → interior preview → expandable curriculum → offer → FAQs → mock checkout.

Why these sections exist

A CSS course interior makes the product tangible without stock photos. Expandable modules let visitors choose their level of detail. The offer groups inclusions and unconfirmed commercial terms in one place. Checkout never asks for payment.

From structure to visual direction

The wireframe starts with the visitor’s question, followed by the information needed to take the next step. The final concept adds a distinct visual language while keeping the action in the same hierarchy.

PropositionOffer context / previewDecision supportPrimary action

Final direction

Lavender, peach and bold sans-serif headings make the course feel like a creative product. The classroom mockup previews the format before the visitor reaches the offer.

Desktop and mobile views

The layout stacks on narrow screens. Align and Forma retain a mobile action bar; Fieldnotes keeps the short form in the main reading flow. Tap a preview to enlarge it.

What I would A/B test

The course interior versus the curriculum as the first supporting section; “Preview checkout” versus “Explore the course”. Measure offer engagement and checkout intent once a real product and valid tracking exist.

These are hypotheses, not reported findings. A real test would need a defined audience, sufficient traffic, a primary metric and a quality check on the leads or purchases.
